# DENSE96: 96 slices at NATIVE 384, from an external leaderboard ablation rather than our own ruler

Registered 2026-10-08, BEFORE the corpus is built. Authorised ("注册并开始96-slice+384").

## Why now, and why the evidence is external
A public write-up ("Reaching 0.949 with one CoAtNet", 2026-10-08) reports a single-model public LB
of 0.949 and, usefully, an ablation table measured ON THE LEADERBOARD. Two rows bear on us:

    94 eval windows reduced to 24          0.940 -> 0.934   (-0.006)
    native inference crop 320 -> 304/288   0.948 -> 0.947/0.946

His starting point -- "our all-data CoAtNet, 80 slices and 78 windows" -- is **0.939**. Our own
all-data CoAtNet is **0.928 (R) / 0.929 (R_swa)**: the same backbone, the same 12-finding
attention-MIL head, a ~0.010 gap on a single model.

Our configuration against his, from `competition_details/reference/dread_0924_inference.py` and
our own kernel:

| | his | ours |
|---|---|---|
| slices | 80 -> 96 | **64** (`SLOTS` 18/14/12/8/12) |
| eval windows | 78 -> 94 | **42** (`K_EVAL = 42`, "every window position the volume holds") |
| cache resolution | **native 384** | **336, then F.interpolate up to 384 at train and eval** |

**We cannot raise the window count by editing the kernel.** 42 is what a 64-slot volume yields;
more windows requires more slices, i.e. a new corpus. Our own notes flagged this a week ago
("the first remaining candidate that changes WHICH ANATOMY is seen is the slice budget 64->96")
and it was never run.

## The treatment: TWO constants, scaled, nothing else
`rsna/data/build_dread_corpus_d96.py`, a copy of the registered builder whose ONLY changes are two
counted, asserted substitutions on the extracted reference source text:

    SLOTS  18/14/12/8/12  ->  27/21/18/12/18     (x1.5, the same proportions; MAXS 64 -> 96)
    IMG    336            ->  384                 (native; removes the 336->384 upsample entirely)

`CROP_MM = 140.0` is UNCHANGED, so the field of view is identical and only the sampling density
changes. Output `dread/train/all_vols_d96.npy` (4407 x 96 x 384 x 384 uint8, ~62 GB; 77 TB free).
Within-slot three-slice windows go from 54 to 86, which is where his 94 comes from.

## THREE CHANGES ARE BUNDLED AND I AM NOT GOING TO ATTRIBUTE THEM
slice density, image resolution, and (consequently) window count all move together. A gain cannot
be assigned to any one of them from this arm. That is deliberate: the question right now is
whether the ~0.010 single-model gap lives in this cluster at all, not which third of it does.
If it passes, attribution is a separate, later question and needs its own arms.

## Judged by the LEADERBOARD, not by gold58
Same discipline as LABCAL-C. gold58 is computed and reported; it does not promote or block.
Its record in this project: LABCAL-B gold58 +0.0074 -> LB 0.000; LABCAL-C gold58 +0.0003 ->
LB -0.003; experiment B gold58 +0.023 -> LB 0.000. Its ordering was right once, its magnitude
never.

    PRIMARY: one full-data seed-42 run on the new corpus, submitted alone.
    Reference: **LB 0.929** (R_swa, our all-data single seed on the 64-slice corpus).
      **>= 0.935**  -> the gap lives in this cluster; fund the second seed + SSL, target ~0.942
      0.929 +- 0.003 -> it does not; the corpus is kept but the line closes
      **<= 0.925**  -> actively worse; close and keep the 64-slice corpus

## THE RISK THAT COULD KILL THIS ON KAGGLE RATHER THAN ON MERIT
Inference cost scales with the window count: 86 windows vs 42 is **~2x the forward passes**, on
top of 1.3x the bytes per window. Our LcR5f kernel (5 CoAtNet arms, 42 windows) ran the 3-study
placeholder in 73 s and the real test in ~2.4 h against a **9 h limit**. A 2-seed + SSL kernel at
86 windows could land at 5-7 h. **Before any submission, the placeholder runtime must be measured
and extrapolated**, and if it exceeds ~6 h the arm ships with one seed or fewer eval windows.
We have already lost one submission to a 9 h timeout (`RGR5f SSL`, 2026-10-02).

## What is deliberately NOT copied from his recipe
His bb_lr 4.01e-5 / head_lr 0.00195 / weight decay 0.0588 (ours: 3e-5 / 1e-3 / 0.02), his
batch-1-with-accumulation-16 schedule, his native-320 inference crop, and his anatomical mirror
TTA. Each is a separate knob; folding them in now would make a pass unattributable to anything.
The mirror TTA (+0.001 in his table) is an inference-only change that can be added later to any
kernel, and is NOT the same as the naive width-flip our own notes forbid -- his own table shows
that naive version failing (0.940 -> 0.939).
External OAI data is not available to us and is not part of this arm; without it his recipe reads
~0.942-0.943, which is the realistic ceiling this line is aiming at.
